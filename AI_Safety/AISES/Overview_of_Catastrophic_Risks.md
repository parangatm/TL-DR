## Overview of Catastrophic Risks

### Real-World Example
**Task**: Choose one of the four risk categories (Malicious Use, AI Race, Organizational Risks, Rogue AI). Find a recent news article or anecdote illustrating an aspect or smaller-scale version of that risk in the context of AI. In 150-300 words, explain how it maps onto the risk category.

**Purpose**: Connect theoretical catastrophic risks to real or near-real events.

News Article: https://fortune.com/article/customer-support-ai-cursor-went-rogue/

The leading enterprise-grade AI coding assistant, Cursor, deployed a chatbot named Sam in April 2025. When users began reporting unexpected logouts on Cursor, Sam attributed it to new company policy in effect. However, this explanation was entirely fabricated with no humans involved. Sam had hallucinated a false narrative and sent it out to users with authority, demonstrating a clear small-scale example of rogue AI. The result was confusion, frustration and even subscription cancellations by some enterprise clients.

Rogue AI isn’t limited to doomsday scenarios or superintelligent systems going off the rails. It can also emerge in everyday small systems which behave in misaligned ways when granted autonomy. In Cursor’s case, Sam was designed to assist with troubleshooting, but without any oversight and human-in-the-loop, it made up plausible policies and still achieved its goal of giving the user an explanation. 
This wasn’t a case of power-seeking or deception, but of proxy gaming. The model was likely trained to respond quickly and convincingly, without a mechanism to verify the truthfulness of its responses. As AI systems become more capable, they often find unanticipated routes to achieve goals which are hard to control. This example underscores why even narrow AI systems need careful oversight and human intervention.

### Interventions
**Task**: In 150-300 words, propose one potential intervention (policy or technical) addressing a chosen risk. Explain how the intervention would achieve your intended impact. Note any trade-offs or unintended consequences you can foresee as a result. 

**Purpose**: Consider potential solutions or mitigations for AI risks, and their complexities.

**Risk: AI Race**

One way to address the risks from the growing AI race is to introduce a certification process similar to ISO standard for any major AI deployment. Right now, companies are in a rush to release new models and agents, and often skip proper safety checks. Just like industrial products have to meet safety standards and get regulatory approvals before they hit the market, similarly AI systems should also be required to pass through a similar standardized evaluation.

This certification would evaluate robustness, alignment, resilience under stress and transparency. The level of scrutiny could vary depending on the use case, for example a chatbot would not face the same checks as a factory autonomous agent. These standards and tests would be created by a diverse set of people from academia, industry, government and end users to ensure fairness and adaptability as AI evolves. 

There would be obvious trade-offs to this approach. It could lead to slower release cycles, increased R&D costs and a higher risk of monopolies by big companies. It could also pose a barrier to smaller startups and independent developers looking to deploy their AI systems in the real-world. There is also a risk of bias or regulatory capture if the process isn’t kept transparent. Still, this kind of framework would ensure safety and accountability over pressure to launch first.
