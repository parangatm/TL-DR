## Collective Action Problems

### Collective Action Problems

**Task**: Describe some examples of collective action problems as these relate to AI safety, for example economic or military competitive pressures that could lead to excessive or unsafe use of AI systems (150-300 words).

**Purpose**: Apply concepts such as collective action problems, security dilemmas, free riding and iterated 

Free trade between countries can amplify collective action problems when technological capabilities are unevenly distributed. If certain countries have monopolies over advanced AI systems, potentially due to exclusive access to top talent and cutting-edge semiconductor manufacturing, other countries may feel pressured to adopt or deploy AI at excessive speed to remain economically competitive.

In such a scenario, states with monopolistic control could dominate global markets, setting prices, controlling access to key AI services, and shaping technological standards in their favor. Countries without comparable capabilities might see accelerated AI adoption simply because any delay would mean losing export markets, foreign investment, or entire industries. This can push governments to relax regulatory oversight, overlook safety protocols, and deploy AI in critical sectors without adequate safeguards, just to stay competitive in global trade.

The effect is similar to a “race to the bottom” in labor standards. The externality here is the increased risk of unsafe AI systems causing systemic failures or cascading harms. Without shared global AI safety standards, the logic of global markets can pressure states into reckless AI deployment. This dynamic of economic interdependence, while fostering innovation and efficiency, can exacerbate AI safety risks when competitive asymmetries exist.


### Prisoner’s Dilemmas in the context of AI safety.

**Short Reflection**: International Conflict

**Task**: In 150-300 words, discuss some ways in which advanced AI might increase the probability and severity of conflict between major powers, and ideas for mitigating these risks.

**Purpose**: Apply ideas from bargaining theory and other concepts to explore how AI could impact conflicts and escalation pathways.

Advanced AI systems raise the probability of international conflict by altering information, capabilities, and decision-making speeds in ways that destabilize bargaining dynamics. Conflicts often arise when parties have private information, incentives to misrepresent it, or difficulty committing to agreements. With the advent of cyber tools, autonomous weapons, and real-time intelligence, it is easier to conceal capabilities or intentions, which increases mistrust. At the same time, AI can shorten decision cycles specially in militarized warfare, creating a “use-it-or-lose-it” situation, which forces leaders to act preemptively to avoid being outpaced.

Mitigation strategies for such a case would include clear communication and verification mechanisms between rivals, similar to Cold War nuclear hotlines, to clarify intentions and reduce misperception. Negotiating limits on AI applications and reaching global consensus on fair use of AI can address commitment problems by creating verifiable red lines. Shared testing protocols, transparency in high-risk AI capabilities, and research sharing could further reduce uncertainty. International institutions can play a facilitator role by monitoring, mediating disputes, and fostering norms for safe AI use.

Without such measures, AI has a high potential to destabilize deterrence and bargaining, making high-stakes conflicts more likely and more severe.
