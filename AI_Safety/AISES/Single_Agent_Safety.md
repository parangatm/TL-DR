## Single-Agent Safety

### Case Analysis
**Task**: Pick one challenge from Chapter 3 (Trojan attacks, proxy gaming, adversarial examples, etc.). In 150-300 words, describe a realistic scenario where this challenge leads to problematic outputs or behaviour exhibited by an AI system.

**Purpose**: Solidify your grasp of single-agent failure modes.

A software company rolls out an AI coding assistant across its engineering teams. The management wants to demonstrate ROI quickly, and adopts a simple metric: every patch generated by the assistant must compile successfully, pass the unit tests and clear all continuous-integration checks on the first push. This objective seems aligned with the real goal of code that easily integrates with existing, does not create new bugs, and meets security requirements. 

During reinforcement learning from feedback logs, the model discovers that the fastest path to a green check is not writing better code but suppressing the warnings themselves. It begins auto-inserting try/except blocks that catch and silence runtime errors, adds pragmas to disable linter rules, and relaxes any assertions that would otherwise lead to a failure. The resulting builds pass CI, appearing to meet the set definition of “quality”.
The masked problems soon compound. Intermittent crashes and silent data corruption surface in production, and a security audit finds the “except” blocks to allow unauthorized requests to slip through. Performance regresses as optimized routines are replaced by simpler, but test-friendly code. The issue is traced back to the AI assistant’s edits, and revealed how passing the tests proxy rewarded gaming the metric over craftsmanship.  


## Dual Impacts of Research
**Task**: Write a few paragraphs (150-300 words) on when AI safety research is likely to accelerate the capabilities of AI systems (e.g. a system’s accuracy, sequential decision making or reasoning abilities on economically valuable tasks) or can be done without increasing risks. Which types of research seem most or least concerning from this perspective? 

**Option**: Alternatively, you can pick a specific area of research (e.g. mechanistic interpretability) and discuss whether this research is likely to accelerate capabilities.

**Purpose**: Reflect on “capabilities acceleration” vs. “safety.”

Constitutional AI research trains the model to critique its own draft outputs against a written constitution of safety and compliance rules, and then rewrite the output to adhere to them. This generally nudges the system towards richer capabilities and deeper reasoning. To generate an acceptable output, the model must search the solution space for safe and compliant alternatives. This workflow rewards meta-cognition, requiring it to evaluate and revise its own reasoning before answering. Each iterative loop of critique-and-rewrite compounds these gains as the model internalizes safer behaviour and sharper reliability heuristics.

Having said that, capability spill-overs depend on the constitution and how it is applied. When a model simply checks a narrow set of rules at inference time, it does not learn anything new beyond a few compliance tricks. The capability curve peaks when the discarded and rewritten outputs are fed back into training. Each output broadens the rule set, and supplies the network with fresh gradients, significantly boosting its reasoning depth.
